# The Codex

> The Codex preserves the understanding of the Atelier.

It exists so that reasoning need not be rediscovered.

Software inevitably evolves.

Requirements change.

Architectures mature.

Implementations are replaced.

Without a record of understanding, each generation inherits only the current state of the work and must reconstruct the reasoning that produced it.

The Codex exists to prevent that loss.

---

# Purpose

The Codex records the enduring knowledge of the Atelier.

It captures:

- principles
- architecture
- engineering practices
- important decisions
- discoveries
- observations
- questions
- milestones

Its purpose is not merely to document software.

Its purpose is to preserve understanding.

---

# Relationship to the Doctrine

The Doctrine governs principles.

The Codex preserves understanding.

Architecture expresses those principles.

Implementation gives them material form.

The relationship is therefore:

```text
The Atelier Doctrine
        │
        ▼
The Codex
        │
        ▼
Architecture
        │
        ▼
Implementation
```

Implementation may change frequently.

The Doctrine should change rarely.

The Codex provides continuity between them.

---

# Organisation

The Codex is organised by purpose rather than chronology.

## Genesis

The founding understanding of the Atelier.

## Architecture

The structural reasoning behind the platform.

## Engineering

Practices, standards, conventions and guidance.

## Services

External systems integrated into the platform.

## Product

Functional understanding of the platform itself.

## Operations

Deployment, testing and stewardship.

## Future

Ideas that have not yet matured into doctrine or implementation.

## Observatory

Observations, experiments and discoveries.

## Apprenticeship

Guidance for future artisans.

## Leaves

Milestones in the evolution of the Atelier.

---

# Leaves

A Leaf represents a completed stage in the evolution of the project.

Leaves are not versions.

They are chapters.

Each Leaf records:

- why the milestone existed
- what became true
- the principal artefacts
- discoveries
- preserved decisions
- unresolved questions
- guidance for future artisans and agents

Only one Leaf should ever be active.

Completed Leaves coagulate into the permanent history of the Atelier.

---

# Architecture Decision Records

Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) preserve significant technical decisions.

They answer questions such as:

- Why was this chosen?
- What alternatives were considered?
- What consequences follow from the decision?

The implementation may eventually disappear.

The reasoning should remain.

---

# Stewardship

Every contributor inherits the work from those who came before.

The responsibility of stewardship is not merely to add.

It is to leave the work more coherent than it was found.

Documentation should therefore evolve through rectification rather than accumulation.

When understanding improves, the Codex should improve with it.

---

# Guidance for Future Artisans and Agents

Before making significant changes:

1. Read the Doctrine.
2. Read the latest completed Leaf.
3. Read the current active Leaf.
4. Read relevant Architecture Decision Records.
5. Understand the existing implementation.
6. Rectify only when coherence increases.

Do not infer architectural intent from implementation alone.

The implementation is evidence.

The Codex preserves understanding.

---

# Closing Reflection

The Codex is not the software.

It is the memory of the software.

It exists so that understanding may endure beyond any individual artisan, implementation, or generation of the platform.

Every contribution should leave that memory clearer than it was before.
