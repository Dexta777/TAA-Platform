# Doctrine Evolution

> A record of the conceptual rectifications through which the Atelier Doctrine becomes more coherent.

## Purpose

The Doctrine is living, but it is not casual.

Foundational language may be rectified when practice reveals a clearer expression, a stronger distinction, or a more coherent principle.

This document preserves those moments.

It does not record every editorial change. It records the evolution of understanding.

Its purpose is to help future artisans—and future agents—understand not merely what the Doctrine says, but why it came to say it.

---

## The Nature of Rectification

Every doctrine begins imperfectly.

Understanding is discovered through observation, inquiry, practice, and reflection. As understanding deepens, language must sometimes be refined so that it more faithfully expresses the principle it intends to preserve.

Rectification is therefore not revision for its own sake.

It is the deliberate act of bringing an idea into greater coherence.

A doctrinal rectification should:

- emerge from practice rather than novelty
- increase coherence
- clarify responsibility
- preserve useful historical context
- strengthen judgement
- resist unnecessary change

The Doctrine should always lag behind practice.

No principle should become doctrine because it is elegant.

It should become doctrine only after it has repeatedly proved itself worthy of preservation.

# Rectifications

## Rectification 001 — Method became Doctrine

### Earlier Expression

The Atelier Method

### Rectified Expression

The Atelier Doctrine

### Reason

A method describes how a task is performed.

The Atelier is concerned with something more fundamental: the enduring principles through which artisans exercise judgement, preserve coherence, and accept responsibility for the work entrusted to them.

The word _Doctrine_ better reflects this role.

### Consequence

The hierarchy of authority became:

```text
The Atelier Doctrine
        │
        ▼
The Codex
        │
        ▼
Architecture
        │
        ▼
Implementation
```

Doctrine governs principle.

The Codex preserves understanding.

Architecture gives structure to principle.

Implementation gives it material form.

---

## Rectification 002 — Followers became Artisans

### Earlier Expression

The Doctrine seeks followers.

### Rectified Expression

The Doctrine cultivates artisans.

### Reason

The Atelier does not teach conclusions that must be accepted.

It cultivates the ability to observe, inquire, reason, decide, and accept responsibility.

A follower is defined by agreement.

An artisan is defined by judgement.

### Consequence

Reasoned disagreement is compatible with the Doctrine when exercised honestly and in service of greater coherence.

The Atelier does not prescribe what to think.

It seeks to cultivate how to think.

---

## Rectification 003 — Invitation became an Open Workshop

### Earlier Expression

The Doctrine seeks fellow artisans.

### Rectified Expression

The Atelier extends an open workshop.

### Reason

Even "fellow artisans" subtly implies belonging.

The Atelier does not recruit.

It offers a place where thoughtful people may practise together.

### Consequence

Participation requires neither allegiance nor identity.

Anyone may enter, examine the work, contribute, question, improve, or depart.

The workshop remains open.

---

## Rectification 004 — Leaves became Milestones

### Earlier Expression

Individual documents were regarded as Leaves.

### Rectified Expression

A Leaf represents a completed stage in the evolution of the Atelier.

### Reason

Documents are artefacts.

Leaves are milestones.

Confusing the two would bind the history of the Atelier to individual files rather than meaningful stages of understanding.

### Consequence

Leaf I encompasses Genesis itself:

```text
Leaf I — Genesis
├── First-Leaf.md
├── Doctrine.md
├── Glossary.md
└── Doctrine-Evolution.md
```

Documents may continue to evolve long after the Leaf in which they first appeared has coagulated.

---

## Rectification 005 — Leaves became Context Capsules

### Earlier Expression

Leaves summarised completed milestones.

### Rectified Expression

Leaves preserve operational context for both future artisans and future agents.

### Reason

Large bodies of work should never rely upon memory alone.

The history of reasoning must remain discoverable.

Future contributors require a dependable path into the thought behind the work.

### Consequence

Each Leaf should preserve:

- Purpose
- What Became True
- Artefacts
- Principles Discovered
- Decisions Preserved
- Open Questions
- State at Coagulation
- Guidance for Future Artisans and Agents

The Codex remembers.

The Leaves provide orientation.

---

## Rectification 006 — Versioning became Rectification

### Earlier Expression

Foundational documents might simply receive new versions.

### Rectified Expression

Foundational documents evolve through Rectification.

### Reason

Versioning records that something changed.

Rectification records that understanding became more coherent.

The distinction is philosophical rather than procedural.

### Consequence

Every doctrinal change should preserve its reasoning.

Future readers should inherit understanding rather than merely chronology.

---

## Rectification 007 — Doctrine follows Practice

### Earlier Expression

Principles could be written in anticipation of future usefulness.

### Rectified Expression

The Doctrine should lag behind practice.

### Reason

Ideas imagined ahead of experience often become ornamental.

The Atelier should first observe what consistently produces clarity, resilience, coherence, and responsibility.

Only then should that understanding be elevated into doctrine.

### Consequence

The TAA Platform becomes the proving ground of the Doctrine.

Practice reveals patterns.

Observation tests them.

Rectification clarifies them.

Doctrine preserves them.

---

# Current Understanding

The Atelier Doctrine is intentionally unfinished.

It should never become complete.

It should become increasingly coherent.

Every rectification should answer a single question:

> Does this leave the Doctrine more capable of cultivating sound judgement and coherent work?

If the answer remains uncertain, the idea should remain within inquiry rather than doctrine.

---

# Guidance for Future Rectifications

Before recording a doctrinal rectification:

1. Identify the earlier expression.
2. Record the rectified expression.
3. Explain what practice revealed.
4. Describe the increase in coherence.
5. Record the consequences.
6. Preserve historical context respectfully.
7. Never change language merely because it appears more elegant.

Rectification exists to preserve understanding.

Not to rewrite history.
